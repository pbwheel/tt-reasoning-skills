# Cross Domain

## Metadata

- Category: Solve
- Primary gap: `solution_gap`
- Input requirements: a target problem whose objective, constraints, and important mechanism can be abstracted
- Questions: Conditional—ask when the target outcome or constraints determine whether an analogy transfers; follow the [questioning policy](../../protocols/questioning-policy.md)
- Evidence policy: [shared evidence policy](../../protocols/evidence-policy.md), treating transferability as inference rather than fact
- Recommended predecessors: `first-principles` when industry language or inherited assumptions hide the structure
- Recommended successors: `expert-panel` for multidisciplinary critique or `minimum-experiment` to test a translated mechanism
- Compatible with: `first-principles`, `expert-panel`, and `minimum-experiment`

## Abstract

Remove domain-specific nouns and describe:

- actors or components;
- scarce resources;
- flows and feedback loops;
- objective;
- constraints;
- failure modes;
- time scale and reversibility.

The abstraction must preserve the mechanism that matters. If it becomes so generic that everything is analogous, refine it.

## Search for analogues

Choose several meaningfully distant domains with mature responses to the same structure. Prefer structural diversity over a list of near-synonyms.

For each analogue:

1. describe its original problem and mechanism;
2. map elements and causal relationships to the target;
3. identify the conditions that make the mechanism work;
4. identify where the analogy breaks;
5. translate the mechanism into a target-domain option.

Compare translated options against the target's real constraints. An evocative metaphor without a usable mapping is not a result.

## Evidence

Follow the [evidence policy](../../protocols/evidence-policy.md). Verify claims about source domains when they are material. Treat transferability as an inference and state its confidence.

## Output contract

Include:

- the domain-neutral problem structure;
- selected analogues and selection rationale;
- explicit structural mappings;
- transferable mechanisms and non-transferable details;
- adapted solution options;
- risks and a way to test the most promising transfer.

## Termination

Stop when additional domains no longer add a distinct mechanism or change the target solution space.
