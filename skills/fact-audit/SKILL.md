# Fact Audit

## Metadata

- Category: Research
- Primary gap: `evidence_gap`
- Input requirements: one or more identifiable claims, arguments, or conclusions to audit
- Questions: Q0—proceed with the claims available; follow the [questioning policy](../../protocols/questioning-policy.md)
- Evidence policy: [shared evidence policy](../../protocols/evidence-policy.md), strictly preserving `FACT != INFERENCE`
- Recommended predecessors: `deep-research` only when a broad landscape is needed to identify the decision-critical claims
- Recommended successors: `steelman-decision` when the audited premises support a live choice
- Compatible with: `socratic-clarify` when claim ambiguity prevents decomposition

## Method

Decompose the material into:

- externally verifiable claims;
- inferences from those claims;
- value judgments or recommendations.

Build a claim ledger for every material proposition. Give each factual claim one verdict:

- verified;
- broadly supported but needs narrowing;
- disputed;
- insufficient evidence;
- contradicted.

Then audit the reasoning even when the premises are true. Check hidden assumptions, correlation-versus-causation, selection effects, denominator or baseline errors, omitted alternatives, time mismatch, and conclusions stronger than the evidence.

## Source discipline

Use sources capable of establishing each claim. Prefer primary and current sources, record dates when relevant, and do not cite a source for a proposition it does not support. Show consequential source conflicts.

## Output contract

Include:

- the claim ledger with evidence and verdicts;
- separation of fact, inference, and judgment;
- the strongest valid conclusion supported by the evidence;
- the largest reasoning defect;
- a corrected or narrowed version of the original claim;
- current confidence and unresolved unknowns.

## Termination

Stop when all decision-relevant claims and inference links have a verdict. Do not expand into a general survey unless the audit exposes a broad evidence gap that truly blocks the conclusion.
