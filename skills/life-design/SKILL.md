# Life Design

## Metadata

- Category: Self
- Primary gap: `direction_gap`
- Input requirements: enough of the user's current state, values, strengths, constraints, and desired life conditions to construct distinct paths
- Questions: QN—multi-turn, one high-value question at a time; follow the [questioning policy](../../protocols/questioning-policy.md)
- Evidence policy: [shared evidence policy](../../protocols/evidence-policy.md), treating future paths as hypotheses rather than predictions
- Recommended predecessors: `talent-discovery` when strengths or energizing conditions are unclear
- Recommended successors: `steelman-decision` for a mature path choice or `minimum-experiment` for a reversible probe
- Compatible with: `talent-discovery`, `steelman-decision`, and `minimum-experiment`

## Build the design brief

Establish enough of:

- current commitments and dissatisfaction;
- values expressed through actual choices;
- strengths and energizing conditions;
- financial, health, family, geographic, legal, and time constraints;
- desired work and non-work conditions;
- acceptable downside and reversibility.

Do not require exhaustive autobiography. Ask only what changes the path space.

## Generate futures

Create multiple genuinely different paths, not cosmetic variants of one preferred answer. Each path should specify:

- what daily life could look like;
- which values and strengths it expresses;
- assumptions required for it to work;
- major trade-offs and opportunity costs;
- early warning signs;
- reversible probes that produce real evidence.

Include continuity paths as well as change paths when plausible. Avoid prestige-based ranking and deterministic claims about what the user “should” become.

## Output contract

Include:

- concise current-state and design criteria;
- material constraints and uncertainties;
- at least two meaningfully different future paths;
- trade-offs and required assumptions for each;
- comparison without pretending values are objective facts;
- small reversible tests and what each would teach;
- the next reflection question only if it remains blocking.

## Termination

Stop when the user has a small portfolio of plausible futures and a safe next experiment, rather than a falsely certain life verdict.
