# Personal Development Examples

## Discover strengths, then paths

**Request:** “I do not know what I am best at or what career to try next.”

**Path:** `talent-discovery -> life-design -> minimum-experiment`

`talent-discovery` gathers concrete episodes and looks for recurring behavior, outcomes, energy, and enabling conditions. `life-design` uses supported patterns, values, and constraints to construct several meaningfully different futures. `minimum-experiment` turns the most informative uncertainty into a reversible real-world probe.

Do not label the user from one anecdote, rank paths by prestige, or treat a short experiment as proof of a permanent identity.

## Choose between known paths

**Request:** “I already have two viable paths: management or staff engineering. Help me choose.”

**Path:** `steelman-decision -> minimum-experiment`

If strengths, constraints, and options are sufficiently known, do not restart talent discovery or life design. Identify the strongest conditions for both paths and the variable most likely to reverse the choice. When possible, test that variable with a temporary leadership responsibility, shadowing arrangement, or time-bounded project.

## An unclear desire for change

**Request:** “I feel stuck and want a different life.”

**Path:** `socratic-clarify`, then reassess

Clarify what “stuck” refers to, what the user wants more or less of, which facts and interpretations are being mixed, and what constraints are real. Do not jump directly to career options before the actionable problem is known.
